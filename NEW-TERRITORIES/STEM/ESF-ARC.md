# ESF Sha Tin College: Introduction to Architecture (Ages 14-16)
## Course Development & Meeting Preparation

---

## Executive Summary

This document outlines a pioneering secondary school architecture course designed for 14-16 year-old students at ESF Sha Tin College. The course bridges the gap between secondary education and professional architectural practice, providing students with a well-rounded introduction to architectural thinking, design process, sustainability, and practical applied design technology.

**Course Philosophy:** Architecture is not simply a profession; it is a way of thinking about the world, about people, the environment, society, and how we live together. This course introduces students to that way of thinking while developing creative, critical, and civic skills applicable across all disciplines.

## Proposed 12-Week Course Structure

### **Week 1-2: Foundations of Architectural Thinking**

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand what architecture is and its role in society
- Develop design thinking and problem-solving mindsets
- Recognize how buildings shape human experience and behavior
- Explore the relationship between form, function, and meaning

**Key Topics:**
- What is architecture? Beyond buildings to systems thinking
- Design thinking methodology: empathize, define, ideate, prototype, test
- The built environment and its impact on society, culture, and well-being
- Basic design principles: form, function, proportion, balance, rhythm, harmony
- Case studies of iconic buildings and their design processes

**Activities:**
- Guest speaker: Practicing architect discussing career pathways and design philosophy
- Analysis of familiar spaces: How does your school building make you feel?
- Design thinking workshop: Redesign a space in the school
- Sketching exercises: Developing visual communication skills
- Field observation: Walking tour of Sha Tin analyzing urban design

**Assessment:**
- Reflective journal on design thinking process
- Sketches and observations from field work
- Participation in design thinking workshop

---

### **Week 3-4: Design Process & Representation Methods**

**Learning Objectives:**
- Master fundamental design representation techniques
- Understand the design process from research to presentation
- Develop proficiency in both analog and digital design tools
- Learn to communicate architectural ideas effectively

**Key Topics:**
- The design process: research, analysis, concept development, refinement, presentation
- Sketching and visualization techniques: hand drawing, perspective, orthographic projection
- 2D representation: plans, sections, elevations, axonometric drawings
- 3D representation: physical models, digital 3D modeling
- Introduction to digital design tools: SketchUp, Rhino basics
- Site analysis and contextual research methods

**Activities:**
- Sketching intensive: Perspective drawing, orthographic projection
- Physical model building: Understanding 3D form through making
- SketchUp tutorial: Creating simple 3D models
- Site analysis project: Analyzing a local site in Sha Tin
- Design project: Personal space redesign (bedroom, study area, or locker)

**Equipment & Tools:**
- Sketching materials (pencils, paper, rulers, set squares)
- Model-making materials (cardboard, foam, wood, adhesives)
- SketchUp software (free educational version)
- Measuring tools and site analysis templates

**Assessment:**
- Portfolio of sketches demonstrating progression
- Physical model of personal space redesign
- Digital 3D model in SketchUp
- Site analysis report with drawings and photographs

---

### **Week 5-6: Bioclimatic Design & Sustainability**

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand climate-responsive architecture principles
- Analyze Hong Kong's unique climate challenges
- Design buildings that minimize environmental impact
- Appreciate the relationship between sustainability and good design

**Key Topics:**
- Climate science basics: temperature, humidity, wind, solar radiation
- Passive design strategies: natural ventilation, daylighting, thermal mass, shading
- Active systems: mechanical ventilation, heating, cooling, renewable energy
- Hong Kong's climate challenges: tropical heat, humidity, typhoons, dense urban environment
- Green building standards: LEED, BREEAM, BEAM Plus (Hong Kong standard)
- Case studies: Sustainable buildings in Asia (Singapore, Japan, China)
- Bioclimatic design pioneers: Hassan Fathy, Renzo Piano, Norman Foster

**Activities:**
- Climate analysis workshop: Understanding Hong Kong's climate data
- Passive design simulation: Testing ventilation and daylighting strategies
- Building performance analysis: How do buildings respond to climate?
- Field trip: Visit a sustainable building in Hong Kong (e.g., HSBC headquarters by Foster + Partners)
- Design project: Climate-responsive pavilion for Sha Tin College campus
  - Site analysis considering sun path, wind patterns, views
  - Passive design strategies for comfort
  - Material selection for durability and sustainability
  - Integration with landscape and existing campus

**Case Study Buildings:**
- **HSBC Headquarters, Hong Kong** (Norman Foster): Iconic example of climate-responsive design in Hong Kong
- **Supertrees, Singapore** (Wilkinson Eyre): Bioclimatic design and vertical greening
- **Nakagin Capsule Tower, Tokyo** (Kisho Kurokawa): Sustainable modular design
- **Bullring Shopping Centre, Birmingham** (Future Systems): Sustainable urban design

**Assessment:**
- Climate analysis report with diagrams
- Passive design strategy sketches and calculations
- Climate-responsive pavilion design with drawings and model
- Reflection on sustainability principles

---

### **Week 7-8: Materials, Structure & Technology**

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand how buildings stand up and stay standing
- Explore material properties and their design implications
- Appreciate the relationship between structure and form
- Integrate technology thoughtfully into architectural design

**Key Topics:**
- Building materials: concrete, steel, wood, glass, brick, stone
- Material properties: strength, durability, sustainability, aesthetics
- Structural systems: compression, tension, bending, torsion
- Common structural types: beams, columns, arches, trusses, frames, shells
- How buildings resist loads: gravity, wind, earthquakes
- Digital fabrication: 3D printing, CNC cutting, laser cutting
- Technology in contemporary architecture: smart buildings, automation, sensors
- Material innovation: sustainable materials, recycled content, bio-based materials

**Activities:**
- Material exploration workshop: Testing material properties
- Structural model building: Understanding load paths and stability
- Bridge design challenge: Build a bridge from limited materials
- Digital fabrication exploration: 3D printing or laser cutting a design element
- Hands-on workshop: Model building with sustainable materials (recycled, natural)
- Guest speaker: Structural engineer or materials scientist

**Experiments:**
- Cantilever beam testing: How far can materials extend?
- Arch strength testing: Comparing different arch shapes
- Material durability: Weathering and aging of different materials
- Structural efficiency: Comparing weight-to-strength ratios

**Assessment:**
- Material property research and presentation
- Structural model demonstrating understanding of load paths
- Bridge design and construction
- Reflection on material and structural choices in design

---

### **Week 9-10: Urban Design & Community Impact**

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand architecture's role in creating livable communities
- Analyze urban planning and public space design
- Appreciate architecture's social and cultural significance
- Develop awareness of heritage conservation and adaptive reuse

**Key Topics:**
- Urban planning fundamentals: density, mixed-use development, walkability
- Public space design: streets, parks, plazas, gathering spaces
- Community building through architecture and urban design
- Adaptive reuse and heritage conservation
- Social responsibility in design: accessibility, inclusivity, equity
- Hong Kong's urban challenges: density, heritage preservation, green space
- Urban design case studies: successful public spaces and community projects

**Activities:**
- Urban analysis project: Mapping Sha Tin's urban fabric
  - Density analysis
  - Land use mapping
  - Public space inventory
  - Pedestrian flow patterns
  - Heritage and cultural landmarks
- Field trip: Walking tour of Sha Tin analyzing urban design
  - Identifying successful and unsuccessful public spaces
  - Understanding how urban design affects community life
  - Documenting architectural heritage
- Community engagement workshop: How do we design for people?
- Design project: Community space proposal for Sha Tin
  - Identifying a community need or opportunity
  - Site analysis and stakeholder engagement
  - Design concept addressing social and environmental goals
  - Presentation to community stakeholders

**Heritage & Cultural Sites in Hong Kong:**
- **HSBC Headquarters** (Norman Foster): Modern architectural icon
- **Star Ferry Terminals**: Historic transportation infrastructure
- **Tian Hou Temple, Sha Tin**: Traditional Chinese architecture
- **Sha Tin Town Hall**: Modernist civic architecture
- **Hong Kong Museum of History**: Understanding Hong Kong's built heritage

**Assessment:**
- Urban analysis maps and diagrams
- Field trip observations and photography
- Community space design proposal with drawings and model
- Presentation to design professionals and community members

---

### **Week 11-12: Capstone Project & Industry Engagement**

**Learning Objectives:**
- Synthesize learning from entire course into comprehensive design project
- Develop professional presentation and communication skills
- Engage with practicing architects and design professionals
- Explore career pathways and further study options
- Reflect on personal learning and growth

**Key Topics:**
- Design project management and workflow
- Professional presentation skills: drawings, models, verbal communication
- Design documentation and portfolio development
- Career pathways in architecture and related fields
- Further study options: university programs, internships, professional development
- Ethical practice and professional responsibility

**Activities:**
- Student-led design projects addressing real community needs
  - Identifying a design challenge in Sha Tin or ESF community
  - Comprehensive site analysis and research
  - Design development with multiple iterations
  - Integration of sustainability, community impact, and technical excellence
  - Professional-quality drawings, models, and documentation
- Industry mentorship sessions: One-on-one guidance from practicing architects
- Design review sessions: Feedback from design professionals
- Portfolio development: Documenting design process and outcomes
- Final presentations to design professionals, school community, and stakeholders
- Career exploration panel: Architects discussing their paths and specializations
- Reflection workshop: Personal learning, growth, and future directions

**Capstone Project Options:**
1. **Community Space Design:** Public plaza, community center, or gathering space
2. **School Facility Design:** New classroom, library, or student center
3. **Sustainable Housing:** Affordable housing or student accommodation
4. **Cultural Heritage Project:** Adaptive reuse of historic building
5. **Urban Renewal:** Revitalization of underutilized urban area

**Assessment:**
- Comprehensive design project with research, analysis, and design development
- Professional-quality drawings, models, and digital presentations
- Written design statement explaining design decisions
- Verbal presentation to design professionals and community
- Portfolio documenting entire course learning
- Reflective essay on personal growth and future directions

## Conclusion

This Introduction to Architecture course represents a pioneering educational initiative that bridges secondary education and professional architectural practice. By grounding the curriculum in design thinking, sustainability, and practical applied design technology, we create a learning experience that connects the dots between science, technology, engineering, art, and mathematics through the lens of architecture.

The course honors the wisdom of leading international architectural education programs while remaining deeply rooted in Hong Kong's unique context and opportunities. Through experiential learning, industry mentorship, and engagement with real community challenges, students will develop not only technical skills but also the creative, critical, and civic thinking that defines architectural practice.

Most importantly, this course recognizes that architecture is fundamentally about creating spaces that enhance human life and well-being. By introducing students to this way of thinking at an early stage, we equip them with tools and perspectives valuable not only for those pursuing architecture professionally, but for anyone seeking to understand and shape the built environment.
