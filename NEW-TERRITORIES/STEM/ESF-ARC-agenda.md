# ESF Sha Tin College: Introduction to Architecture
## Meeting with Paul Clark – Tomorrow Morning

---

## How We're Thinking About This

We've been reflecting on the Design Day energy and the conversation with Paul about launching an architecture course. What struck us most was the genuine hunger from students—they want to understand how buildings work, why cities are designed the way they are, what it means to create spaces that matter.

We're coming into this conversation wanting to understand what Paul's really trying to build. What does success look like for him? What are the constraints he's working within? Once we understand that, we can figure out how we might be useful.

---

## Opening the Conversation (First 15 minutes)

We'll start by asking Paul to paint the picture. What's the vision? We're genuinely curious about:

- What does he want students to walk away with? Not just "understanding architecture," but what actually changes in how they think?
- What are the real constraints? Timetabling, budget, space, staffing—what's he working with?
- Who are the students he's hoping to reach? Is this for the architecture-curious, or a broader group?
- How does this fit into his bigger vision for the design faculty?

We'll listen more than we talk here. There's no point in us pitching ideas if we don't understand what he's actually trying to do.

---

## Understanding the Practical Reality (Next 10 minutes)

Once we understand the vision, we need to get real about logistics:

- What space does he have? A studio? A classroom? Outdoor areas?
- What's already in place? Computers, software, fabrication tools?
- What's the budget looking like? Materials, guest speakers, field trips?
- Who on his team might be involved? Is he teaching this solo, or is there a team?
- When does this need to be ready? September launch means we're working backwards from June.

These aren't deal-breakers—they're just the reality we're working within. Better to know now than discover halfway through that we've designed something that doesn't fit.

---

## Exploring What We Might Offer (Next 15 minutes)

Once we understand what Paul needs, we can talk about what we might bring to the table. We're thinking about this in a few ways:

**On curriculum:** We could help develop lesson plans, design projects, assessment frameworks. We've spent time looking at how leading schools approach this—Bartlett, AA, IAAC—and we could help translate that into something that works for Sha Tin.

**On industry connections:** We know architects, structural engineers, sustainability consultants. We could facilitate guest speakers, organize site visits to buildings like the HSBC headquarters, potentially connect students with real projects they could contribute to.

**On teaching:** Depending on what Paul needs, we could lead certain modules—maybe the sustainability weeks, or the capstone project mentoring. Or we could work alongside his team, supporting them as they teach.

**On mentorship:** We could pair with students on their final projects, give feedback, help them think through design decisions.

But honestly, we won't know what's most useful until we understand what Paul's actually trying to do. Maybe he needs curriculum design help. Maybe he needs industry connections. Maybe he needs someone to teach the technical stuff while he focuses on the bigger picture. We'll figure that out together.

---

## Questions Worth Exploring (Next 15 minutes)

As the conversation develops, we'll probably want to dig into:

**On the course itself:**
- Is this a 12-week intensive, a semester course, or something that runs all year?
- How many hours per week? What's realistic given everything else happening at the school?
- Is it an elective, or integrated into existing subjects?
- How many students? We're thinking 15-20 is ideal for hands-on work, but what does Paul think?

**On what students actually do:**
- What skills matter most? Sketching? Digital tools? Sustainability thinking? Community engagement?
- Should they produce a portfolio? A capstone project? Both?
- How much hands-on making? Model building, fabrication, that kind of thing?
- Should they engage with real community projects, or is this more exploratory?

**On the bigger picture:**
- Is this a pilot, or is Paul planning to run it every year?
- Is he thinking about this as a pathway for architecture-interested students, or a broader STEAM initiative?
- Could this become something other schools want to replicate?

**On practical stuff:**
- What's the timeline? When does he need the course finalized?
- Who else needs to sign off? Curriculum committee? Senior leadership?
- When can we meet again to start developing curriculum in detail?

---

## What We're Hoping to Achieve

By the end of this conversation, we'd like to:

- Understand what Paul's really trying to build and why it matters to him
- Know what constraints we're working within
- Have a sense of where we might be most useful
- Agree on next steps—probably a follow-up meeting to start developing curriculum
- Feel like we're genuinely partnering on something, not just executing a brief

---

## What We're Not Doing Today

We're not asking Paul to commit to anything. We're not asking for budget details. We're not asking him to make decisions about staffing or student recruitment. We're just trying to understand his vision and explore whether we can be useful partners.
