## A1.2.(x) Specific approach and measures to achieve the Environmental Performance Requirements during the construction of the Works and the Operation

The JV is unequivocally committed to achieving and, where feasible, exceeding all stipulated Environmental Performance Requirements (EPRs) for I-PARK2 during both the construction and operational phases. Our approach is holistic, integrating cutting-edge technology, rigorous management systems, and a culture of environmental responsibility to deliver a facility that sets new benchmarks for environmental excellence in Hong Kong. The EPRs, derived from the approved EIA Report, Environmental Permit (EP), contractual obligations, and relevant legislation, will be the minimum standard The JV strives to surpass.
The JV's Overarching Strategy for Achieving EPRs:
Our strategy is built on proactive planning, meticulous execution, continuous monitoring, and adaptive management. Key pillars include:

- **Design for Compliance and Excellence:** Incorporating EPRs into the fundamental design of the facility and construction methodologies.
- **Best Available Techniques (BAT):** Deployment of BAT for all critical environmental control systems.
- **ISO Certified Management Systems:** Leveraging ISO 14001 (Environmental Management) and ISO 14064 (GHG Management) frameworks for systematic control and continual improvement.
- **Robust EM&A:** A comprehensive Environmental Monitoring and Audit programme to track performance against EPRs and trigger corrective actions.
- **Skilled and Trained Workforce:** Ensuring all personnel are competent and committed to achieving EPRs.
Specific The JV Measures to Meet Environmental Performance Requirements:
Our strategy is broken down by project phase to address the unique challenges of each period.

### A1.2.(x).1 Construction Phase EPR Achievement

During the construction phase, The JV will implement a robust Environmental Monitoring and Audit (EM&A) program, managed by our on-site EHS Department and overseen by the Independent Environmental Checker (IEC), to ensure strict adherence to all EPRs.

- **Air Quality:**
  - **Dust Control:** We will implement an advanced dust monitoring and suppression system, including real-time TSP meters linked to automated water sprayers on haul roads and active work areas. All stockpiles will be covered, and any concrete batching plants will be fully enclosed with high-efficiency fabric filters.
  - **Emissions from Machinery:** The JV commits to using only regulated Non-Road Mobile Machinery (NRMM) compliant with the latest standards (Euro VI equivalent) and fueled with B5 diesel, as detailed in section "A1.2(ix).1.a". A rigorous inspection and maintenance program will ensure all machinery operates within its specified emission limits.
  - **Air Quality Monitoring Stations (AQMS):** The JV will provide complete AQMSs in compliance with Specification Part C, Clause 7.1.1.2, based on the requirements in EPD's QA/QC Manuals. These stations will employ analysers based on USEPA Federal Reference Method or equivalent for gases such as SO2, NOx, O3, CO, and for RSP/FSP. Heavy metals and dioxin analysis will follow USEPA Method TO-9A or equivalent. The air sampling system will comply with EPD's QA/QC Manuals. A meteorological monitoring system will also be provided.

- **Noise Control:**
  - **Quiet PME:** We will prioritize the use of Quality Powered Mechanical Equipment (QPME) and other silenced machinery. All PME will be regularly maintained to minimize noise generation.
  - **Acoustic Mitigation:** Stationary noise sources will be enclosed, and mobile noise barriers will be strategically deployed to protect nearby sensitive receivers. For any marine piling, advanced noise attenuation measures like bubble curtains will be used.
  - **Construction Noise Permits (CNPs):** The JV acknowledges that certain operations, particularly those linked to 24-hour requirements for emergency teams and Service Manager facilities (e.g., generator operation at night), may necessitate work outside normal daytime hours. For such activities, The JV will proactively apply for and secure all necessary Construction Noise Permits (CNPs) as required by the Noise Control Ordinance (Cap. 400). All conditions stipulated in the CNPs will be strictly adhered to, and all reasonable measures will be undertaken to minimize noise impacts on sensitive receivers during these periods.
  - **Monitoring:** Continuous, real-time noise monitoring will be conducted at designated points to ensure construction noise levels remain within the strict limits set by the Environmental Permit and relevant CNPs.

- **Water Quality Protection:**
  - **Site Runoff Management:** The JV will implement a comprehensive temporary water management system for the construction phase, centred around a large-scale site detention system to handle runoff, particularly during extreme rainfall. All collected water will be directed to a multi-stage on-site wastewater treatment plant. Crucially, this temporary plant will be specifically equipped to treat contaminants associated with on-site materials, including heavy metals, boron, and aluminium from any disturbed Pulverised Fuel Ash (PFA), ensuring all discharges are fully compliant with the Water Pollution Control Ordinance (WPCO).
  - **Marine Works and Marine Mud Handling:** For marine construction activities, particularly bunker excavation and seawall works, The JV acknowledges the presence of a marine mud layer (typically 2-4m thick, potentially mixing with alluvium). To prevent marine contamination, all excavation and filling activities involving marine mud will be conducted within fully enclosed silt curtains, and non-dredged methods will be prioritized where feasible. Specific disposal prohibitions related to marine mud will be strictly adhered to, with no marine disposal of contaminated mud. Regular water quality monitoring will be conducted both inside and outside the curtains to ensure their effectiveness and compliance with marine discharge standards.
  - **Chemical and Spoil Management:** All chemical waste and excavated spoil (including marine mud) will be handled, treated, and disposed of in strict accordance with EPD regulations and relevant guidelines. A rigorous trip-ticket system will be implemented for full accountability of waste, ensuring proper segregation, storage, and authorized disposal or reuse pathways.

#### A1.2.(x).1.3 Temporary Site Water Management & Extreme Rainfall Contingency

The JV is acutely aware of the significant challenges posed by major rainfall events during the construction phase, as evidenced by historical experiences at similar sites (e.g., STF). Our strategy is designed to effectively manage site water, prevent flooding, and ensure environmental compliance even under extreme weather conditions.

- **Flood Prevention and Water Retention:** The JV will implement a robust temporary site drainage plan early in the construction phase, prior to the wet season. This includes the strategic placement of temporary dams or retention ponds (similar to the STF dam in the SW corner) to collect and manage large volumes of surface runoff, preventing widespread site flooding witnessed historically (e.g., 900mm rain in a month wet season).
- **Runoff Quality Management & Discharge:** All collected site water will be treated to meet the stringent Water Pollution Control Ordinance (WPCO) discharge license limits prior to any discharge. The JV will pro-actively obtain and maintain the necessary WPCO discharge licenses, including provisions for controlled discharge to sea, as permissible under conditions such as Specification A Annex 2 Appendix A1.2 Condition 20. The JV will liaise with the Environmental Protection Department (EPD) to emulate the accommodating approach seen in previous projects, where discharge to sea under WPCO license was permitted following major rainfall events.
- **Water Reuse for Dust Suppression:** Recognizing that water supply services may be limited during construction, particularly after major events, collected and treated site water will be prioritised for general site use, including dust suppression across the site formation, piling, and substructure works. This strategic reuse will minimize reliance on external water sources and enhance overall water resource efficiency.
- **Proactive Drainage Installation:** Understanding that the site's topography can impede natural drainage, The JV will fast-track the installation of temporary comprehensive drainage measures. This includes temporary channels, sumps, and pumping systems implemented as early as practicable, well in advance of the onset of heavy rainfall, to prevent standing water and associated environmental nuisances.
- **Emergency Preparedness:** Our Emergency Procedures Plan will include specific protocols for extreme rainfall events, covering intensified water management, pollution control actions, and communication with the Engineer (ER) and EPD for timely approvals and coordination.
- **Supplementary Water Supply:** In accordance with Specification Part A, Clause 2.4.4(b), should the Water Supplies Department (WSD) supply be insufficient during the construction of the Works, The JV will take all reasonable measures to provide sufficient quantity of water to make up the deficit. This includes the provision of tanker water from external sources, particularly for dust suppression (e.g., PFA dust suppression), wheel washing, and the filling of tanks as needed, to ensure continuous and compliant operations.

### A1.2.(x).2 Stack Emission Monitoring Management (Exceeding Standards)

Advanced Continuous Real-Time Monitoring System (CEMS)
The JV will install and operate a state-of-the-art CEMS, providing 24-hour, real-time, high-precision measurement of all regulated parameters. In accordance with Specification Part C, Clause 2.10.1.2, every two duty CEMS units will be complemented with at least one online hot standby CEMS unit, including the sampling line. The CEMS will measure pollutants as specified in Specification Part C, Clause 2.10.1.13(b), including: particulate matter, HCl, HF, SO2, NOx, CO, and NH3 (to monitor for slippage), as well as O2, water content, temperature, pressure, and flue gas mass flow to determine reference conditions. This comprehensive system will feature:

- **Automated Calibration & Drift Verification:** An automatic calibration program will verify zero and span drift at least hourly. If deviations exceed preset stringent limits (tighter than standard requirements), calibration will be triggered automatically, ensuring maximum data accuracy.
- **Redundancy and Predictive Maintenance:** Key CEMS components will have built-in redundancy, and a predictive maintenance schedule based on sensor diagnostics will minimize downtime and ensure continuous reliable data.
- **Proactive Emergency Response Mechanism for Potential Exceedances:**
  - The JV will implement a sophisticated, multi-level early warning system (proven effective in The JV's operational projects). Real-time emission data will be continuously analyzed by a dedicated system. By predictive calculation, it will estimate if any hourly average value is likely to approach or exceed the stringent EPR limits. This information will be instantly transmitted to duty operational and EHS personnel via SMS, dedicated APPs, and control room alarms, providing crucial lead time for proactive adjustments.
- **Levels of Alert:** For example: Level 1 (80% of limit value) – heightened monitoring and operational check; Level 2 (90% of limit value) – pre-alarm, initiate specific load adjustment protocols; Level 3 (100% of limit value) – full emergency response, immediate load reduction or controlled shutdown if necessary, and EPD notification.
  - The main plant control system will be configured to automatically initiate controlled reductions in incinerator load or other corrective actions when emissions approach pre-set internal alert limits, well before regulatory limits are breached.
  - A comprehensive operational manual will detail technical adjustments, diagnostic checks, and reporting procedures for each potential exceedance scenario, ensuring swift and effective response.
- **Enhanced Maintenance and Support Measures for CEMS:**
  - A rigorous preventive maintenance schedule will be executed, mandating proactive replacement of critical CEMS components (e.g., sampling probes, optical lenses, analyzers) based on operational hours and performance diagnostics, rather than just reactive replacement.
  - The JV will maintain an extensive on-site stock of critical CEMS spare parts—sensors, pump valves, electronic boards, etc.—to ensure rapid replacement and minimize any data gaps.
  - An accredited laboratory will be engaged at least quarterly (or more frequently if indicated by CEMS performance) to perform independent manual stack sampling (isokinetic sampling) and compare these reference method results against automated CEMS data for comprehensive quality assurance and validation, ensuring the CEMS consistently meets or exceeds EPD and international accuracy standards.

### A1.2.(x).3 Odour Control Management (Striving for an "Odour-Free" Environment)

Total Full-Process Negative-Pressure Control
The JV will maintain a robust negative pressure, typically between "“50 Pa to "“100 Pa (or as optimized during commissioning for maximum effectiveness), in the waste reception hall, all waste storage bays/bunkers, MRF processing areas, and other potentially odorous zones to prevent any fugitive odour escape.
An advanced airflow"“balance monitoring system, with multiple differential pressure sensors, will continuously track pressure differentials across all critical zones, linked to the central control system for real-time alerts and automated adjustments to the ventilation system.
High-capacity, energy-efficient induced-draft fans with built-in N+1 redundancy will be installed. The JV will implement reinforced, predictive maintenance protocols for the entire ventilation system and its subsystems (including ductwork integrity checks) to minimize downtime and ensure consistent negative pressure. Extracted odorous air will be primarily used as combustion air for the incinerators, ensuring thermal destruction of odour compounds, with secondary treatment (e.g., activated carbon filters, bio-scrubbers) for any bypass or specific streams. **These odour control systems will be designed to achieve a minimum of 99% odour removal efficiency, a minimum of 99% removal efficiency for hydrogen sulphide (H2S) (or not exceeding 0.1 ppm), and a minimum of 95% removal efficiency for ammonia (NH3) (or not exceeding 5 ppm) before discharging to the atmosphere, in accordance with Specification C Clause 2.12.1.2.**
Furthermore, in accordance with Specification Part D, Clause 1.5.4, The JV commits to maintaining the proper function of all ventilation, dust control, and deodorisation systems. Filter media and scrubbing chemicals will be tested at intervals not greater than three months (as per Specification Part D, Clause 1.5.5), and replaced as required based on manufacturer’s recommendations to ensure optimal performance.
Proactive Sniff Testing and Olfactory Monitoring:
The JV will assign specifically trained and calibrated personnel (olfactory panel) to conduct regular perimeter odour-sniff tests according to a scientifically designed schedule based on the Environmental Management Plan and prevailing meteorological conditions. All findings (intensity, character, duration, location) will be meticulously recorded, and results promptly reported to the Operations Manager and EHS Department for immediate investigation if any off-site odour attributable to I-PARK2 is detected.
Responsive Community Engagement Mechanism for Odour:

- The JV will establish a dedicated 24-hour multilingual complaint hotline (Cantonese, Mandarin, English) managed by trained personnel. Upon receipt of any odour complaint, an immediate on-site investigation protocol will be triggered, including checks of plant operations, negative pressure systems, and off-site verification by the EHS team.
- A proactive odour patrol schedule will be implemented, with trained The JV staff conducting perimeter inspections around the plant boundary at least twice daily (e.g., morning and evening, plus additional patrols during specific meteorological conditions conducive to potential odour migration), maintaining detailed logs of observations.
- The JV will publish a monthly odour-management performance report for the community and EPD, transparently disclosing monitoring data (e.g., number of patrols, any complaints received and their resolution status) and any corrective actions taken, fostering public confidence.

### A1.2.(x).4 Particulate Control at the IBA Treatment Plant (Minimizing Fugitive Dust)

#### A1.2.(x).4.1 Fully Enclosed IBA Handling System

- The entire Incineration Bottom Ash (IBA) handling and treatment process (from ash quench to storage of processed material) will be carried out in a fully enclosed system. Double-layer heavy-duty soft curtains, airlocks, and other effective environmental protection measures will be installed at all material transfer points, vehicle loading/unloading bays, and building openings to prevent fugitive dust emissions.
- Advanced fogging/misting dust suppression devices, using fine water mist, will be strategically installed and automatically activated during IBA crushing, screening, and conveying processes to maintain optimal moisture content and suppress dust generation at source.

#### A1.2.(x).4.2 Guaranteed Emission Compliance Assurance for IBA Plant

- The JV will install a high-efficiency dust collection system for the IBA plant, potentially a combination of robust bag filters and/or wet electrostatic precipitators, designed to ensure the outlet particulate concentration is consistently less than 5mg/m'³ (or a more stringent site-specific EPR if applicable).
- A rigorous "three shifts with two rotations" operational monitoring system will be implemented for the IBA plant, with The JV staff conducting at least three 30-minute visual dust emission checks and equipment performance verifications per shift.
- The JV will establish and maintain a strict control standard for the moisture content of IBA before and during treatment, dynamically adjusting water addition via fogging systems to maintain it within the optimal dust suppression range (e.g., 18-22%, to be confirmed during commissioning).

#### A1.2.(x).4.3 Proactive Equipment Maintenance and Management for IBA Plant Dust Control

- The integrity of all dust removal filter bags (or other critical filtration media) will be checked daily through differential pressure monitoring and visual inspections where accessible. All filter bags will be replaced immediately if the overall damage or performance degradation rate exceeds a conservative threshold (e.g., 5% or as per manufacturer's peak performance guidelines).
- Air volume monitoring devices within the dust extraction system will be calibrated weekly to ensure that the air volume deviation of each branch pipe and extraction point is less than 10% of the design specification, guaranteeing effective dust capture.
- The system pressure difference curve across filters will be evaluated monthly (or more frequently via automated trending). The filter bag cleaning program (e.g., reverse pulse jet) will be automatically triggered if the pressure difference abnormally increases by a set percentage (e.g., 20%), indicating filter loading.

### A1.2.(x).5 Water Quality Management (Protecting the Marine Environment)

#### A1.2.(x).5.1 Advanced Classification and Treatment of Wastewater

- The JV will implement a complete diversion and separate collection system for all wastewater streams: leachate from MSW storage, process wastewater from flue gas scrubbers and boiler blowdown, cooling tower blowdown, water from site cleaning and vehicle washing, MRF process water, and domestic sewage. Each stream will be directed to the appropriate pre-treatment or main treatment train within the on-site WWTP. In accordance with Specification Part C, Section 2.15 and Clause 2.1.6.11, the Wastewater Treatment Plant will be designed for continuous operation, ensuring reliable and continuous treatment of wastewater in full compliance with effluent discharge and reuse standards.
- The on-site WWTP will adopt a state-of-the-art combined process, potentially including "physical & chemical pre-treatment + advanced biological treatment (e.g., MBR) + tertiary membrane treatment (e.g., RO/NF) and disinfection," designed to ensure the final effluent consistently meets or exceeds the stringent first-level standards of the Water Pollution Control Ordinance (WPCO) and any specific discharge license conditions. The design will emphasize energy efficiency, water conservation, and reliable water supply under emergency conditions, with a focus on automatic operation and telemetry integration with the SCADA/DCS system.
- The JV will construct an adequately sized emergency storage pool/tank with a capacity sufficient to hold the maximum anticipated wastewater production for at least 72 hours (or as per EP requirements) in case of WWTP malfunction or downstream discharge restrictions.

#### A1.2.(x).5.2 Comprehensive Seawater Monitoring and Protection

- The JV will deploy a network of at least four (or as specified in the EM&A Manual) online, real-time water quality monitoring buoys at strategic locations around the I-PARK2 discharge point(s) and at sensitive marine receiver locations. These buoys will continuously monitor key indicators such as pH, Dissolved Oxygen (DO), turbidity, temperature, and salinity.
- An intelligent, tidal adaptive discharge protocol will be implemented for treated effluent. Wastewater discharge will be automatically controlled, potentially suspended or significantly reduced during sensitive tidal periods (e.g., low dispersion periods during slack tides) to maximize initial dilution and minimize any potential localized impact.
- The JV will conduct regular (e.g., monthly or quarterly, as per EM&A Manual) marine ecological surveys, including monitoring of benthic biological communities, coral health, and other sensitive indicators, to proactively assess and ensure no adverse impacts from I-PARK2 operations.

#### A1.2.(x).5.3 Robust Anti-seepage and Leak Prevention System

**Preserving Infrastructure Integrity and Environmental Safety:**

- **Protective Layer Maintenance:** The JV is fully aware of the existing membrane layer and 35MPa grout mattress located under the seawall (except the west seawall), installed during reclamation to prevent PFA contamination into the sea. The JV guarantees that this critical protective layer will be identified, protected, and kept meticulously intact throughout all works, especially during site formation, piling, and substructure activities. Any accidental damage will be immediately reported and rectified using approved methods to maintain its integrity and prevent PFA migration.
- **PFA Contamination Management (Wastewater Treatment):** The JV acknowledges the presence of heavy metals (e.g., chromium), boron, and aluminum in PFA within the STF formation. Any PFA-leachate or other contaminated water streams encountered during construction will be managed by our dedicated temporary wastewater treatment system. This system will incorporate specialist treatment processes (e.g., chemical precipitation, advanced oxidation, and/or ion exchange) specifically designed to effectively remove these contaminants, ensuring full compliance with all discharge standards before reuse or release.
- **Comprehensive Leakage Detection:** A comprehensive leakage detection system, potentially including interstitial monitoring sensors or negative pressure monitoring probes within double-layer containment structures or pipe galleries, will be installed for early warning of any potential leaks.
- **Regular Integrity Testing:** The JV will conduct regular (e.g., weekly or as appropriate) integrity tests of critical anti-seepage layers using approved methods (e.g., electric spark detection for geomembranes, hydrostatic testing for tanks) during construction and commissioning, and periodically during operation.

### A1.2.(x).6 Waste Management (Championing Circular Economy Principles)

#### A1.2.(x).6.1 Incineration Bottom Ash (IBA) Resource Utilization

- The JV will implement highly effective screening and metal recovery technologies (advanced magnetic and eddy current separators) within the IBA processing plant to achieve a metal (ferrous and non-ferrous) recovery rate of '‰¥ 95% from the raw IBA, ensuring continuous treatment of I1 IBA (from I•PARK1) and I2 IBA (from the Facility). The design will comply with the specific quality requirements for IBA treatment systems outlined in Specification Part C, Clause 2.1.3 and the stringent standards for ash-derived aggregates defined in Specification Part C, Clause 2.1.4. The IBA Treatment Plant will feature two symmetric processing lines, each capable of operating at a minimum of 75% of the required daily treatment capacity, ensuring redundancy and operational flexibility during normal operation and maintenance.
- A stringent quality-control system will be established for IBA destined for reuse as aggregate or in construction products. This will include daily testing of heavy-metal leachate concentrations (e.g., TCLP tests) and other relevant physical/chemical properties to ensure full compliance with all applicable standards (e.g., CSWRI Technical Circular No. 1/2017 or subsequent EPD guidelines).
- All transport vehicles for IBA (whether for reuse or disposal) will be equipped with GPS tracking and real-time monitoring. Loads will be fully sealed/covered throughout transit to prevent spillage or dust.

#### A1.2.(x).6.2 Hazardous Waste (IFA/APC Residues) Control

- The JV will provide dedicated, purpose-built, and compliant storage facilities for IFA and APC residues, strictly in accordance with the Code of Practice on Chemical Waste Disposal and all DGO requirements. This includes secure, bunded, and covered storage areas.
- A rigorous consignment-note (trip-ticket) transfer system will be implemented for all hazardous waste movements, utilizing dedicated, licensed vehicles for transportation to EPD-approved final disposal sites.
- The JV's EHS Department will conduct monthly internal compliance audits covering all aspects of hazardous waste management, from generation and on-site storage to transportation and final disposal records.

#### A1.2.(x).6.3 Overall Waste Minimization at Source

- The JV will optimize the combustion control system continuously to ensure an exceptionally low ash loss-on-ignition (LOI) rate, consistently < 3%, indicating very high combustion efficiency and minimizing unburnt carbon in the IBA.
- We will strengthen upstream source management (liaising with waste collectors/transfer stations) and optimize internal consumable usage controls (e.g., for FGT reagents) to reduce overall waste generation.
- Fly ash stabilization/solidification treatment will be meticulously controlled, with chelating-agent and cement dosing dynamically adjusted based on daily (or batch-specific) test results of the raw fly ash characteristics to ensure effective immobilization and minimize reagent use.
- A comprehensive, digital waste-tracking ledger will be established and maintained to record in real-time the generation, on-site handling, treatment, and final destination (reuse, recycling, or disposal) of all waste streams generated by I-PARK2.

  - **Film Showing Hall:** Provides a dedicated space with noise-reduction features for educational videos on WtE processes and development, with new content provided every two years.
  - **Recycled Art Gallery:** Showcases art exhibits made from recycled materials, updated bi-annually to maintain public appeal.
  - **Digital Immersive Lab:** An interactive lab aligned with themes of the Facility's purpose, Hong Kong's waste management, climate change, and environmental protection awareness.
  - **Recycling Workshop:** Offers varied workshops (at least three per year, with different themes) to encourage public participation in recycling and sustainable practices. Themes and content will be updated bi-annually.
  - **Photo-op Area:** Provides themed photo opportunities related to the Facility, updated every two years.
  - **Waste Management Learning Area:** A comprehensive and engaging learning environment equipped with digital displays, multimedia presentations, interactive games, informational panels, learning stations, information kiosks, and simulated recycling stations to educate visitors on waste reduction, recycling, and sustainable living. Exhibits will be updated at least every two years.
- **Public Access and Engagement:** Facilities ensure provisions for easy access for visitors, including those with disabilities, with clear way-finding and comfortable viewing areas. The integrated visitor path connects educational and operational areas, offering panoramic views where possible.
- **Community Relation:** The JV ensures communication officers are available to lead site tours and carry out other public relations work, engaging fluently in Putonghua, Cantonese, and English.
