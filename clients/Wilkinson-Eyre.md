# Wilkinson Eyre

## Practice Overview

Wilkinson Eyre is an internationally renowned architecture practice founded in 1983 by Chris Wilkinson, later joined by Jim Eyre in 1987. Based in London with offices in Hong Kong and Sydney, the firm has established a global reputation for its innovative and elegant designs across a diverse range of sectors. Wilkinson Eyre is distinguished as the first architecture practice to win the prestigious RIBA Stirling Prize in consecutive years, for the Magna Centre in Rotherham (2001) and the Gateshead Millennium Bridge (2002).

## Design Philosophy

Wilkinson Eyre's approach to architecture is characterized by:

- **Contextual Sensitivity**: Creating buildings that respond thoughtfully to their surroundings
- **Structural Expression**: Celebrating engineering and structural elements as integral to design
- **Technological Innovation**: Embracing advanced materials and construction techniques
- **Environmental Responsibility**: Integrating sustainable strategies from concept to completion
- **Spatial Experience**: Crafting memorable spaces that enhance human experience
- **Light and Transparency**: Using light as a fundamental design element

## Sustainability Approach

Wilkinson Eyre integrates sustainability throughout its design process:

- **Passive Design Strategies**: Optimizing building orientation, form, and envelope to reduce energy demands
- **Innovative Environmental Systems**: Implementing cutting-edge technologies for energy efficiency
- **Material Selection**: Choosing materials based on lifecycle assessment and environmental impact
- **Biodiversity Enhancement**: Incorporating living systems and green infrastructure
- **Adaptive Reuse**: Reimagining existing structures to extend their useful life
- **Climate Resilience**: Designing for future climate scenarios and changing conditions

## Design Process

The practice's design methodology includes:

- **Collaborative Approach**: Working closely with clients, engineers, and specialists
- **Iterative Development**: Refining designs through multiple stages of exploration
- **Physical Modeling**: Using models at various scales to test and communicate ideas
- **Advanced Digital Tools**: Employing parametric design and BIM technologies
- **Material Research**: Investigating innovative materials and applications
- **Post-Occupancy Evaluation**: Learning from completed projects to inform future designs
