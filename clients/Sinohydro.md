# Sinohydro Corporation

## Company Overview

Sinohydro Corporation, now operating as PowerChina Sinohydro, is a Chinese state-owned hydropower engineering and construction company. As a subsidiary of Power Construction Corporation of China (PowerChina), Sinohydro has established itself as one of the world's leading companies in hydroelectric power project contracting and construction. Beyond hydropower, the company has diversified into various infrastructure sectors including transportation, buildings, water works, and renewable energy projects across the globe.

## History

Founded in 1950, Sinohydro was initially established to build hydropower projects within China. The company played a pivotal role in developing China's domestic hydropower capacity, participating in the construction of most major dams in the country.

Key milestones in Sinohydro's history include:

- **1950s**: Establishment and focus on domestic hydropower projects
- **1970s-1980s**: Expansion of technical capabilities and project scale
- **1990s**: Beginning of international operations
- **2000s**: Rapid global expansion, particularly in Africa and Southeast Asia
- **2011**: Restructuring and listing on Shanghai Stock Exchange
- **2012**: Incorporation into PowerChina as a key subsidiary
- **Present**: Operating as PowerChina Sinohydro with global operations in over 80 countries

## Notable Projects

### Three Gorges Dam (China)

Sinohydro was a major contractor for the world's largest hydropower project, with a capacity of 22,500 MW.

### Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam (Ethiopia)

A 6,450 MW project on the Blue Nile, one of Africa's largest hydropower developments.

### Bakun Hydroelectric Project (Malaysia)

A 2,400 MW project featuring a 205-meter-high concrete-faced rockfill dam.

### Bui Dam (Ghana)

A 400 MW hydroelectric project on the Black Volta River.

### Coca Codo Sinclair (Ecuador)

A 1,500 MW hydroelectric facility, Ecuador's largest energy project.

### Kariba North Bank Extension (Zambia)

A 360 MW extension to the existing Kariba North Bank power station.
