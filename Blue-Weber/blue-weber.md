# Blue Webber

Blue Weber Agave was named for the German botanist, Franz Weber, who studied the flora of the region in the last decade of the 19th century. In 1949, the Mexican government established this species of agave to be the main agave in the production of tequila.

### The Opportunity: Why Tequila, Why Asia, Why Now

**Global Tequila Growth**

Premium and super-premium tequila is moving from niche to mainstream global luxury. The market reached USD $25.67 billion in 2025 and is forecast to reach USD $39.52 billion by 2030, representing a 9.01% CAGR (Mordor Intelligence, 2025). IWSR projects global tequila volume growth at +7% CAGR from 2021–2026, versus +2% CAGR for overall spirits, while super-premium-and-above tequila is expected to account for 55% of global tequila value by 2026, up from 33% in 2016 (IWSR, 2022). Asia-Pacific is also the fastest-growing tequila region globally, with double-digit volume growth (Mordor Intelligence, 2025).

Tequila's trajectory is comparable to Scotch whisky because both are protected origin categories — Jalisco and Scotland — with regulated aging classifications that create scarcity, provenance, and collectability. In both cases, the market has shifted from commodity consumption to sipping and investment-led demand, especially as Asian collectors have driven premiumization. The Bonhams Hong Kong whisky auction boom from 2010–2023, led by Daniel Lam, proved how quickly Asia can transform a spirits category into a serious collectible asset class.

**Why Hong Kong Specifically**

Hong Kong is the natural gateway for premium spirits into Asia. It operates as a free port — no tariffs, no duties on spirits in transit — making it the lowest-friction entry point for premium imported goods. Its auction infrastructure is world-class, its collector community is sophisticated and liquid, and its geographic position places it within hours of China, Singapore, Japan, Korea, and Southeast Asia. The city's bilingual business environment (English and Cantonese/Mandarin) is uniquely suited to bridging Mexican brand authenticity with Asian consumer culture.

**The Asia Gap**

Despite this global surge, Asia remains almost entirely unserved by dedicated tequila infrastructure. There is no dedicated tequila auction house on the continent. There is no specialist tequila broker serving family offices and cask investors. There is no vertically integrated tequila platform connecting Mexican distilleries directly to Asian collectors, investors, and consumers. This is not a gap — it is an open territory.

**Asian collectors are actively rotating out of whisky and into aged tequila. The window to establish category leadership is open now — but it will not remain open indefinitely.**
