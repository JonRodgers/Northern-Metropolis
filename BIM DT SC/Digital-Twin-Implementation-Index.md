## COMMON QUESTIONS & ANSWERS

**Q: What is the difference between a Digital Twin and a BIM model?**
A: A BIM model is a static representation of a building's design and construction information. A Digital Twin is a dynamic, real-time virtual representation that integrates live operational data from sensors and systems, enabling predictive analytics and optimization.

**Q: Do I need to follow ISO 19650 to implement a Digital Twin?**
A: Yes. ISO 19650 provides the foundation for high-quality BIM data that is essential for a successful Digital Twin. The data quality, standards, and governance established through ISO 19650 compliance ensure that the Digital Twin has reliable, accurate information.

**Q: What BMS systems are supported?**
A: The BMS Integration Guide covers Honeywell, Johnson Controls, Siemens, Schneider Electric, and Trane. Other systems can be integrated using the same principles and patterns described in the guide.

**Q: How long does implementation take?**
A: A typical implementation follows a 12-month roadmap (4 phases of 3 months each). However, timeline can vary based on facility complexity, existing systems, and resource availability.

**Q: What is the cost of implementation?**
A: Costs vary based on facility size, complexity, and scope. Key cost drivers include: Autodesk Tandem licensing, sensor procurement and installation, BMS integration development, and staff training. Typical ROI is achieved within 2-3 years through energy savings and maintenance optimization.

**Q: Can I implement Digital Twin for multiple facilities?**
A: Yes. Phase 4 of the implementation roadmap includes expansion to additional facilities. The architecture and procedures can be scaled to support multiple facilities.

**Q: What happens if my BMS doesn't have an API?**
A: The BMS Integration Guide describes multiple integration approaches, including edge gateways and data scraping, that can work with legacy systems without APIs.

**Q: How do I ensure data security?**
A: The Digital Twin Framework (Section 10) and BMS Integration Guide (Section 2) provide comprehensive security requirements including encryption, authentication, access control, and audit logging.

**Q: What training is required?**
A: Training requirements vary by role. The Autodesk Tandem Workflows document includes training recommendations for different user types (administrators, facility managers, operators, analysts).

**Q: How do I measure success?**
A: Success metrics are defined in the Digital Twin Framework (Section 1) and include: data latency, system uptime, data accuracy, user adoption, cost savings, energy reduction, and maintenance cost reduction.
